# Pantone Challenger V1.3
## Evidence Integrity and Output Quality Repair Specification

**Status:** Implemented in the V1.3.0 repair source; live shadow-run acceptance remains pending  
**Target release:** V1.3.0  
**Primary objective:** Make every published color traceable, visually accurate, correctly attributed, and safe to approve without inspecting raw JSON.  
**Release posture:** Daily publishing remains disabled until all acceptance criteria in this document are met.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Pantone Challenger currently captures live commercial webpages, extracts colors, clusters them across companies, and renders a daily social package. The product concept remains strong, but the August 30 review exposed four trust-breaking problems:

1. Page backgrounds and interface colors can be mistaken for campaign colors.
2. Logos and favicons are displayed as if they are evidence of the selected color, even when the logo itself has no relationship to the color signal.
3. Runtime logo capture produces inconsistent, cropped, undersized, or over-enlarged marks.
4. The output can look polished even when the underlying evidence is not strong enough to support a public claim.

V1.3 changes the unit of analysis from a full webpage screenshot to a **traceable marketing-creative region**. Every company shown as supporting a winner must have a specific captured region and a local color swatch that perceptually matches the winning cluster. Logos become attribution only; they never serve as color evidence.

The system will use three publication states:

- **Ready:** sufficient evidence; create a review pull request and social package.
- **Review only:** a plausible result exists, but confidence or calibration is insufficient; create private artifacts only.
- **Blocked:** the run does not support a trustworthy result; create diagnostics only.

No placeholder brand, preview data, test fixture, favicon, or unverified sector label may appear in a public package.

## 2. Product principles

### 2.1 Evidence before aesthetics

A result must not be published merely because it produces an attractive post. A result is publishable only when a reviewer can trace the winning color to independent source evidence.

### 2.2 A logo is attribution, not evidence

A company logo may identify the source. It must never be used to imply that the company’s logo color caused the winner. Every evidence card must show the **actual local swatch detected in that company’s marketing creative**.

### 2.3 No forced daily winner

“No publish today” is a valid product outcome. Pantone Challenger must prefer a blocked or review-only day over a misleading winner.

### 2.4 One company, one normalized vote

A company may contribute multiple creative regions, but its total daily influence must be normalized. One image-heavy retailer cannot cast dozens of votes against a company with one campaign hero.

### 2.5 Registry metadata is authoritative

Company name, sector, URL, and approved brand mark come only from the source registry. They must not be inferred from page text, logo appearance, or a language model at runtime.

### 2.6 The machine selects; the reviewer validates

A reviewer may reject poor data, rights issues, broken graphics, or a misleading claim. A reviewer may not replace the algorithmic winner with a more attractive color.

## 4. Root-cause analysis

### 4.1 Full-viewport palette extraction is too coarse

The current system extracts colors from two broad screenshots per site. Those screenshots contain headers, navigation, footers, blank space, cookie interfaces, product grids, and brand infrastructure. Even after neutral penalties, a visually large page treatment can outweigh the actual campaign creative.

### 4.2 Evidence is represented by logos instead of matched colors

The evidence Story currently shows company marks and an aggregate adjusted share. A Spotify icon, a black Max mark, or a PlayStation icon does not visually prove that those pages contributed to a green winner. The missing element is the **source-specific matched swatch and the region from which it came**.

### 4.3 Runtime logo scraping is not presentation-grade

Header-image selection and favicon fallback are unreliable. Tiny favicons become enlarged, transparent padding changes optical size, and marks with unusual aspect ratios render inconsistently.

### 4.4 Early-history scoring is underconstrained

During cold start, source baselines are not mature enough to distinguish a changing campaign color from a company’s permanent visual identity. Early results therefore require stricter gating and a calibration period.

### 4.5 Output validation checks structure, not truthfulness

The renderer can successfully create a polished image even when the source metadata, local evidence, or color selection is weak. V1.3 adds pre-render integrity checks.

## 5. Architecture changes

### 5.1 New unit of analysis: `EvidenceRegion`

Add a structured evidence-region model:

```python
@dataclass
class EvidenceRegion:
    source_id: str
    frame_id: str
    region_id: str
    selector_hint: str
    region_type: str
    screenshot_path: str
    bbox: tuple[int, int, int, int]
    viewport_area_ratio: float
    image_area_ratio: float
    text_density: float
    confidence: float
    eligible: bool
    rejection_reason: str
```

Each public source contribution must reference one eligible `EvidenceRegion`.

### 5.2 New local evidence model: `CandidateEvidence`

Replace parallel candidate arrays such as `source_ids`, `source_names`, `source_sectors`, and `source_salience` with a structured evidence list:

```python
@dataclass
class CandidateEvidence:
    source_id: str
    source_name: str
    sector: str
    region_id: str
    region_path: str
    local_hex: str
    local_oklab: tuple[float, float, float]
    distance_to_candidate: float
    local_share: float
    source_weight: float
    region_confidence: float
    page_title: str
    source_url: str
```

A candidate may count a source only when at least one `CandidateEvidence` record satisfies the matching gates.

### 5.3 Candidate model additions

Each candidate must include:

- `family_label`
- `creative_name`
- `confidence`
- `evidence`
- `top_source_weight`
- `top_sector_weight`
- `score_margin_to_next`
- `evidence_region_count`
- `registry_version`
- `methodology_version`

### 5.4 Daily-result publication state

Replace a two-state ready/blocked model with:

```text
ready
review_only
blocked
```

- `ready`: eligible for a daily pull request and social package.
- `review_only`: private reviewer package, no publishable PR.
- `blocked`: diagnostic artifact only.

## 6. Source registry specification

`config/sources.yml` remains the authoritative panel registry. Each source must support these fields:

```yaml
- id: spotify
  name: Spotify
  sector: entertainment
  url: https://www.spotify.com/us/
  enabled: true
  weight: 1.0
  region: US
  frames: 2
  brand_mark_path: assets/brands/spotify.png
  brand_mark_status: approved
  brand_mark_fit: contain
  include_selectors: []
  exclude_selectors:
    - header
    - nav
    - footer
  known_house_colors: []
```

### Registry rules

1. `sector` must be selected from a fixed enum.
2. A source ID must be unique.
3. Every displayed company name and sector must be resolved from this registry.
4. A public logo may appear only when `brand_mark_status: approved`.
5. Missing or unapproved marks use a text-only company label.
6. Runtime favicons are not permitted in public output.
7. The source registry must include a `panel_version` and pass a complete validation test.

### Required sector regression checks

At minimum, tests must assert:

- Spotify → entertainment
- Max → entertainment
- PlayStation → gaming
- Epic Games Store → gaming
- Nike → sports
- Peloton → sports
- Apple → technology
- Sephora → beauty

## 7. Capture and creative-region extraction

### 7.1 Page capture

Continue using a real browser and the configured viewport. Capture the page only after cookie dismissal and a stable load wait.

### 7.2 DOM region discovery

Before taking evidence screenshots, inspect visible elements matching:

- `main`
- `section`
- `article`
- `picture`
- large `img`
- elements with CSS `background-image`
- `video[poster]`
- prominent content containers beneath the page header

Exclude elements inside or matching:

- `header`
- `nav`
- `footer`
- cookie banners
- modal overlays
- chat widgets
- account controls
- logo selectors
- elements smaller than the minimum region area

### 7.3 Region eligibility defaults

A region is eligible when:

- it is visible;
- it occupies at least 4% of the viewport;
- its width is at least 280 pixels and height at least 180 pixels;
- it is not primarily navigation or legal text;
- it is not a logo or icon asset;
- it is not a duplicate of another region from the same source;
- region confidence is at least 0.65.

Capture up to three highest-confidence regions per source. A source still contributes at most one normalized vote to each cross-source candidate.

### 7.4 Fallback behavior

A full-viewport screenshot may be retained for debugging, but it must not create a public source contribution when no eligible creative region was found. Such a source is marked `captured_but_no_eligible_region`.

### 7.5 Evidence contact sheet

For every ready or review-only run, create a private contact sheet containing:

- company name;
- sector;
- region screenshot;
- local matched swatch;
- candidate distance;
- local share;
- region confidence;
- source URL.

This contact sheet is a reviewer artifact and is not automatically included in the public social package.

## 8. Color extraction and candidate scoring

### 8.1 Extract palettes from regions, not logos or whole pages

Run perceptual palette extraction independently on each eligible creative region. Logo assets, favicons, headers, and navigation elements are excluded before extraction.

### 8.2 Per-source normalization

For each source:

1. Calculate swatches for each eligible region.
2. Merge perceptually similar swatches within that source.
3. Normalize the company’s total contribution to 1.0.
4. Retain the strongest local evidence record for each cross-source candidate.

A company with three campaign regions cannot exert triple the influence of a company with one region.

### 8.3 Cross-source clustering

Cluster local swatches in OKLab. A source is counted once per candidate. Each counted source must retain the local swatch and region that justified the match.

### 8.4 Neutral handling

Near-black, white, and gray colors remain in the analytical record but are not public candidates during calibration.

A neutral may become ready only after baseline warmup and only when all are true:

- momentum ratio ≥ 2.0;
- at least 10 independent companies;
- at least 6 sectors;
- at least 70% of contributing evidence comes from eligible creative regions rather than solid structural backgrounds;
- top source weight ≤ 20%;
- top sector weight ≤ 40%.

### 8.5 House-color handling

During calibration, the system must not claim that an enduring brand identity is a daily trend. V1.3 uses two layers:

1. Optional registry `known_house_colors` for obvious fixed interface colors.
2. A learned source baseline after at least five valid historical observations for that source.

House colors are suppressed, not deleted. A persistent brand color can still contribute when its current usage materially exceeds its own baseline.

### 8.6 Candidate score

The score should retain these concepts:

- independent company breadth;
- sector breadth;
- source-normalized prevalence;
- creative-region salience;
- momentum against source history;
- evidence confidence;
- concentration penalties;
- house-color penalties;
- neutral penalties.

The public result must expose the components in `result.json` and the review summary.

### 8.7 Candidate distinctness

Winner and runner-up clusters must be perceptually distinct:

- winner-to-runner distance ≥ 0.045 OKLab;
- runner-to-runner distance ≥ 0.040 OKLab.

Near-duplicate shades are merged or omitted rather than presented as separate runner-ups.

## 9. Naming specification

### 9.1 Separate color family from creative nickname

Every public candidate gets two separate labels:

- **Family label:** deterministic and technically grounded.
- **Creative nickname:** playful and optional.

Example:

```text
Family: Chartreuse
Nickname: Boutique Hotel
Public display: Boutique Hotel Chartreuse
```

The creative nickname must never determine the color family.

### 9.2 Family taxonomy

Use a tested set of families with boundary handling:

- Red
- Red-Orange
- Orange
- Amber
- Yellow
- Yellow-Green
- Chartreuse
- Green
- Teal
- Cyan
- Blue
- Indigo
- Violet
- Magenta
- Rose
- Black
- Charcoal
- Gray
- Light Gray
- White

When a hue sits close to a boundary, use a compound family such as `Yellow-Green` or `Blue-Teal` instead of forcing a misleading label.

### 9.3 Naming quality gates

- Near-neutral colors never use chromatic family names.
- The displayed family must be derived from the actual candidate OKLCH values.
- A creative name may be omitted when it creates confusion.
- Recurrence groups use the stable family/centroid identity, not the playful nickname.

## 10. Logo and attribution specification

### 10.1 No runtime favicon fallback in public assets

Favicons may be stored privately for debugging but are never rendered in public social graphics.

### 10.2 Curated brand marks

Approved marks live in:

```text
assets/brands/
```

Each is manually reviewed once and normalized to a transparent canvas. The source registry controls whether it may be displayed.

### 10.3 Rendering rules

- Use `contain`, never `cover`.
- Never crop a logo.
- Never enlarge a raster mark by more than 2×.
- Maintain at least 16% internal padding.
- A mark must occupy between 25% and 75% of its container after normalization.
- Horizontal wordmarks and square marks use separate target boxes.
- If a mark fails quality validation, render the company name as text instead.

### 10.4 Evidence-card hierarchy

The evidence card must visually prioritize:

1. local matched color swatch;
2. company name;
3. sector;
4. approved mark or text fallback;
5. local evidence metric.

The logo is never the largest element on the evidence card.

## 11. Social-output specification

### 11.1 Feed post

The feed post must show:

- the winning color as the dominant field;
- creative nickname and technically grounded family label;
- HEX value;
- date;
- company pages monitored;
- company pages analyzed;
- companies supporting the winner;
- sectors supporting the winner;
- year-to-date recurrence counter;
- a small confidence label: `High`, `Moderate`, or `Calibration`.

### 11.2 Story 1 — Color reveal

A full-screen color field with:

- winner name;
- family label;
- HEX;
- date;
- recurrence count;
- coverage summary.

### 11.3 Story 2 — Evidence

Show up to eight source cards. Each card must include:

- a clearly visible local swatch taken from that source’s creative region;
- company name;
- authoritative sector;
- approved logo or text fallback;
- local matched HEX;
- a plain-language metric such as `Matched color share in sampled creative`;
- no implication that the logo color itself caused the result.

The Story header should say:

> Companies whose sampled creative contained a close match to today’s winning color.

### 11.4 Story 3 — Why it won

Show:

- analyzed/monitored coverage;
- independent company count;
- sector breadth;
- top-source concentration;
- top-sector concentration;
- score margin over the runner-up;
- baseline status or momentum;
- confidence state.

### 11.5 Story 4 — Runners-up

Each runner-up must show:

- a large swatch rendered directly from its HEX;
- family label;
- creative nickname;
- source count;
- sector count;
- score;
- perceptual distance from the winner.

If fewer than three distinct, qualified runner-ups exist, show fewer than three. Do not fill missing slots with weak or near-duplicate candidates.

### 11.6 Public versus private evidence

Public assets use brand attribution, local swatches, and counts. Private review artifacts may include low-resolution source-region screenshots. Full creative screenshots are not automatically published until a separate rights review is completed.

## 12. Publication quality gates

### 12.1 Panel coverage gates

Default ready thresholds:

- at least 30 of 48 company pages produce eligible evidence;
- at least 9 sectors produce eligible evidence;
- no single sector represents more than 30% of all usable company pages;
- at least 70% of successfully captured pages produce one eligible creative region.

Below these thresholds, the result is review-only or blocked.

### 12.2 Winner gates

A ready winner requires:

- at least 6 independent companies;
- at least 4 sectors;
- at least 4 high-confidence evidence regions;
- mean evidence-region confidence ≥ 0.72;
- average local-to-cluster distance ≤ 0.035 OKLab;
- no displayed evidence distance > 0.050 OKLab;
- top source weight ≤ 25%;
- top sector weight ≤ 45%;
- winner score margin over runner-up ≥ 3.0 points, or explicit `close call` review-only status;
- chroma above the standard display threshold, unless the exceptional-neutral gate passes.

### 12.3 Runner-up gates

Each public runner-up requires:

- at least 5 companies;
- at least 3 sectors;
- perceptual distinctness from the winner and other runner-ups;
- a valid family label;
- at least 3 traceable evidence regions.

### 12.4 Metadata gates

Before rendering:

- every source ID must exist in the registry;
- every displayed sector must match the registry;
- every public logo must be approved;
- every evidence card must have a matched local swatch;
- no placeholder, preview, fixture, or sample data may be present;
- methodology and registry versions must be recorded.

### 12.5 Rendering gates

Automated image tests must verify:

- the center of each winner/runner swatch matches the declared HEX within one RGB unit per channel;
- light swatches have a visible boundary;
- text contrast meets the project’s contrast threshold;
- no text overflows its box;
- logos are not cropped;
- text fallback appears when a mark is missing or invalid;
- singular/plural grammar is correct.

## 13. Calibration and recurrence rules

### 13.1 Calibration period

The first seven valid full-panel runs after V1.3 deployment are `review_only`, even when they otherwise pass the ready gates. They build source baselines and allow visual review without creating an official daily history.

### 13.2 Activation

The product may return to ready publishing after:

- seven valid calibration days;
- at least five observations for 75% of enabled sources;
- no unresolved critical defect from the acceptance checklist;
- manual approval of the source registry and brand-mark set.

### 13.3 Recurrence

Recurrence and year-end summaries count only results that:

- were in `ready` state;
- were merged into `main`;
- have not been reverted;
- use a supported methodology version;
- contain a valid family/centroid record.

Rebuilding the same date must replace, not duplicate, its recurrence contribution.

## 14. Workflow behavior

### Ready

- Write the full archive.
- Render social assets.
- Upload raw and private evidence artifacts.
- Open or update a daily review pull request.

### Review only

- Write a temporary review archive under workflow artifacts.
- Render assets with an `INTERNAL CALIBRATION` label.
- Upload private evidence contact sheets.
- Do not open a publishable daily pull request.
- Do not update recurrence.

### Blocked

- Upload capture report, rejection reasons, and diagnostics.
- Do not render public social assets.
- Do not open a daily result pull request.

The scheduled Daily Challenger workflow remains disabled until V1.3 acceptance is complete. All V1.3 validation runs are manual.

## 15. Test plan

### 15.1 Regression tests from the August 30 incident

The test suite must prove:

1. `#141416` and `#EEEEED` cannot become cold-start public winner/runner-up solely from page structure.
2. A Spotify favicon does not count as green marketing evidence.
3. A black Max mark does not count as black campaign evidence.
4. Spotify, Max, PlayStation, Peloton, Apple, Sephora, and Epic Games display the registry sector.
5. No favicon is rendered in a public evidence card.
6. A company appears behind a green winner only when its captured creative region contains a qualifying local green swatch.
7. Winner and runner swatches render the declared HEX accurately.
8. Near-neutral colors receive neutral names.
9. `1 DAY` and `2 DAYS` render correctly.
10. Preview fixtures cannot be loaded by the production pipeline.

### 15.2 Unit tests

Add tests for:

- region eligibility and rejection;
- source normalization;
- local swatch-to-candidate matching;
- concentration calculations;
- house-color suppression;
- family labels near hue boundaries;
- logo approval and text fallback;
- publication-state decisions;
- recurrence exclusion for review-only, blocked, and reverted results.

### 15.3 Golden-image tests

Maintain deterministic rendering fixtures for:

- dark winner;
- light winner;
- highly chromatic winner;
- missing logo fallback;
- long company name;
- one, two, and three runner-ups;
- calibration label;
- recurrence singular/plural.

Synthetic fixtures are permitted in automated tests only. They must never be packaged as a live or publishable daily result.

### 15.4 Shadow-run tests

Run V1.3 manually for seven consecutive days. Review every day for:

- plausible winner;
- traceable local evidence;
- correct sectors;
- readable logos or text fallback;
- accurate swatches;
- no page-chrome domination;
- no duplicate/near-duplicate runner-ups;
- credible coverage counts.

## 16. Acceptance criteria

V1.3 is complete only when all conditions below are true.

### Data integrity

- Every supporting company traces to an eligible evidence region.
- Every evidence card shows a local swatch within the configured distance of the winner.
- Source names and sectors match the registry with no runtime inference.
- One source contributes at most one normalized vote per candidate.

### Color integrity

- Winner and runner-up swatches visually match their stored HEX values.
- Neutrals do not dominate during calibration.
- Color family labels agree with tested OKLCH classification.
- Runner-ups are perceptually distinct.

### Attribution integrity

- No public favicon fallback.
- No cropped, distorted, or over-enlarged brand marks.
- Missing/unapproved marks use text fallback.
- Logos remain attribution only; the local swatch communicates the color evidence.

### Output integrity

- A reviewer can understand the result without opening JSON.
- Coverage, supporting-company count, sector count, and confidence are visible.
- Review-only and blocked runs cannot accidentally enter recurrence or public history.
- No preview or placeholder data appears in production.

### Operational integrity

- CI is green.
- All regression and golden-image tests pass.
- Seven manual shadow runs complete without a critical defect.
- Daily scheduling is re-enabled only after explicit approval.

## 18. Definition of done

Pantone Challenger V1.3 is done when a reviewer can open one daily package and answer all of these questions immediately:

1. What exact color won?
2. What technically grounded family does it belong to?
3. How many companies and sectors supported it?
4. Which companies supported it?
5. What local color did each of those companies contribute?
6. What sampled creative region produced each contribution?
7. How much of the declared panel was successfully analyzed?
8. Did one company or sector dominate?
9. How close was the runner-up?
10. Is this result ready, calibration-only, or blocked?

If any answer is missing or misleading, the day is not publishable.
